## Sources

- **Source:** Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "How to test responsive search ads (RSA) in Google Ads and Microsoft Ads", https://adalysis.com/blog/how-to-test-responsive-search-ads-rsa-in-google-ads-and-microsoft-ads/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2022-09-16 (page modified 2026-07) · **Claim:** The combinations report shows impressions only, not the metric Google optimises for (anecdotally CTR). Five unpinned headlines shown two at a time = 20 combinations; 15 unpinned headlines = up to 2,940; at 100 impressions per combination that is 294,000+ impressions; three unpinned descriptions ×6, four ×12. Confident reads need "well over 100 impressions per combination". Ad groups with a few hundred impressions/month give inconsistent weekly metrics. Example: the most-served RSA converted at under half the rate of its siblings; pausing it added 200+ conversions in the next 30 days. Pick your own success metric (conversions) and pause losers even when Google serves the "best" ad most. Unpinned-only serving is only suitable at large volume (example ad group: 50,000+ clicks/month, 3+ months of RSAs). · **Evidence:** worked examples with account numbers plus combinatorial maths.
- **Source:** Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "How to find real insights hiding in your RSA data", https://adalysis.com/blog/how-to-find-real-insights-rsa-data/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2026-09-29 · **Claim:** Single-ad-group tests only find the best ad for that targeting; messaging insight needs aggregation across ad groups. Method: pick campaigns (brand and non-brand separate), label the test ads (prefix "ad-"), copy under a second label, change one thing in one set, choose scope (headlines vs descriptions; pinning vs asset presence), segment by campaign type. When CTR and CVR disagree, judge on conversions or conversion value per impression. Worked example: 15 headlines, 4 descriptions, 10,000 impressions/ad group/month → "47 years" to significance at 100 impressions per combination. Example results: ads without keyword insertion beat ads with it on CTR and CVR; "quote" messaging beat "affordable" on conversion value per impression despite lower CVR. Do not treat non-significant results as conclusions; rerun unequal tests. · **Evidence:** account test results (some flagged as not yet significant).
- **Source:** Adalysis docs — "Ad testing checklist", https://docs.adalysis.com/product/inside-adalysis/ad-testing-checklist · **Tier** 1 · **Date** undated · **Claim:** Balanced asset mix (relevance, benefits, authority, CTA); "don't just add 15 headlines because Google allows it"; partially pin H1 and H2 to improve data density; CTR alone rarely tells the story — use conversion per impression; set minimum data and confidence before calling a winner. · **Evidence:** method, no numbers.
- **Source:** Frederick Vallaeys, Search Engine Journal — "How To A/B Test Responsive Search Ads", https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-to-test-responsive-search-ads/429270 · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2021-12-17 · **Claim:** Measure on conversions per impression (CTR × CVR) and always include impression volume. Test messaging first (up to three RSAs per ad group, each on a different value proposition/CTA), positions second (pin winners). Asset labels appeared after ~5,000 top-of-page impressions in 30 days; replace "Low" assets and any asset with no impressions after 2+ weeks. Across 157,000+ ad groups with both formats: RSA vs ETA ~10% better CTR, ~20% lower CVR, ~12% worse conversions per impression, but ~4× the queries; doubling impressions gave 76% more conversions in the example. · **Evidence:** Optmyzr cross-account data.

## Dissenting view
Morten Kristoffersen (SavvyRevenue, writing on the Optmyzr blog, 2025-12-15; opinion, not canon) argues for one RSA per ad group with ~5 headlines and 2 descriptions so data concentrates in one ad, refreshing assets inside it rather than running ad-vs-ad tests. The disagreement is about the unit of iteration: asset swaps inside a single RSA (Kristoffersen) versus labelled ad-vs-ad and cross-ad-group pattern tests (Geddes, Vallaeys). Google's own page pushes a third direction: at least two RSAs per ad group and chasing Excellent Ad Strength.

## What the platform currently does [Google docs]
- [Google docs] About RSAs (support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791): H1, H2, D1 are always shown; H3 and D2 are not guaranteed. Recommends ≥2 RSAs per ad group with Good/Excellent Ad Strength; internal data (15–20 Aug 2025) claims ~6.6% more conversions going from 1 to 2 RSAs and ~3.7% from 2 to 3.
- [Google docs] Campaign-level asset reporting (answer/9781208): the "Performance label" column is deprecated; full asset statistics exist only for dates on or after **2025-06-05**; asset-level CTR/CPC/CPA/ROAS are "directional" because they reflect the combinations an asset served in; asset metrics are non-summable; an asset with zero impressions "for several weeks" should be considered for replacement; evaluate at campaign/asset-group level rather than per asset.
- [Google docs] Google Ads API RSA overview (updated 2026-09-30): 3–15 headlines, 2–4 descriptions; performance statistics are collected on combinations and the system favours combinations that perform well together; automatically created assets (AI Max text customisation) are opted out by default for Search and are not returned in the ad's own asset list.

## Baseline check
Read `google-ads-ad-copy` (monthly checklist: pause Low assets, write replacements; quarterly refresh) and `google-ads-experiments` (sample-size formula, 2–8 week windows). Both rely on the Low/Good/Best labels that Google deprecated in 2025, and neither gives a per-combination data rule, a cross-ad-group pattern method, or a conversion-per-impression decision metric. This skill replaces the label-driven loop with an impression-budgeted, label-based, cross-ad-group loop.

## Judgment calls
- Treated the monthly cadence from the index row as the review cadence, with Geddes' per-combination impression budget deciding whether a given ad group is even eligible for a read that month.
- Chose conversions per impression (Vallaeys 2021, Geddes 2026) as the primary decision metric over CTR, which is what the combinations serving appears to favour.
- Google's "6.6% from a second RSA" is cited as a platform claim, not as a reason.

## Canon strength: Strong
Three Tier 1 practitioners (Geddes/Adalysis, Vallaeys/Optmyzr, Hopkins/Optmyzr) with cross-account studies and worked account examples, 2021–2026.

## Invalidated by
Removal of asset-level clicks/conversions reporting, a change to the 15/4 asset limits or the H1/H2/D1 guaranteed slots, or Google making AI Max text customisation mandatory (which would put non-advertiser assets into every test).
