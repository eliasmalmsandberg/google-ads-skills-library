2. **Source**: Fred Vallaeys (host) with Shawn Walker, Optmyzr PPC Town Hall, "Practical advice on how to actually make Smart Bidding work for you" — https://www.optmyzr.com/ppctownhall/practical-advice-on-how-to-actually-make-smart-bidding-work-for-you · **Tier**: 1 (Vallaeys/Optmyzr; Walker is a guest) · **Date**: 2025-01-29 · **Claim**: Start every account simple and add complexity only as data supports it; split themes into ad groups, not campaigns, because optimisation happens at campaign level; create a separate campaign only when a theme needs its own budget, targeting that cannot be set at ad-group level, or performs very differently (brand vs non-brand); aim for ≥30 conversions per campaign per 30 days rather than Google's 15; below that campaigns become unstable; new low-data campaigns may start on manual bidding. · **Evidence or opinion**: Practitioner opinion with account examples; the 30-conv figure is a rule of thumb, backed by the Optmyzr study above.

3. **Source**: Brad Geddes (Adalysis), "Google Ads Account Organization", workshop slides, Google Marketing Conference (Boussias) — https://presentations.boussiasevents.gr/files/_boussias_conferences_content/presentations/google_marketing_conference/2020/day2/workshop_brad_geddes_googleMKT_20.pdf · **Tier**: 1 · **Date**: 2020 (conference year from URL; slides undated) · **Claim**: Under automated bidding keep all keyword × match-type combinations in one ad group; create a new ad group only when the keyword is not reflected by both the ad and the landing page; separate match types into their own ad groups or campaigns only for budget or different bid method, never for keyword-level bids; structure is "never an absolute" and over-engineering can cause zero ads to show. ## Dissenting view
Brad Geddes (2020) is the dissent: his plumber example (1 campaign / 1 ad group / 532 keywords → 112 ad groups, CPA $300 → $27) shows that consolidation past the point where ad and landing page still match the keyword destroys relevance, and that structure choices like match-type separation are legitimate for budget and bid-method reasons. The disagreement is about *what* is being consolidated: Hagakure advocates (Wenner, Adchieve, Springbok) consolidate campaigns and match types to pool conversion data for Smart Bidding; Geddes and Löhlefink argue ad-group granularity must still follow ad/landing-page relevance, and that Hagakure's ad-copy and negative-keyword losses are real. Lolk and Springbok Case 3 also show the method does nothing where targets are identical or impression share is already high.

## What the platform currently does [Google docs]
- Google Ads Help, "The ABCs of Account Structure" (answer 14752782, data cited from Jan–Feb 2023): recommends moving SKAGs into themed ad groups; one campaign per shared budget/target, one ad group per shared landing page/creative theme; merging all match types into one ad group and "then simplify by just using broad match"; removing device segmentation because Smart Bidding factors in device; segmenting campaigns by business objective only. It gives no numeric thresholds for campaigns, ad groups or keywords per ad group.
- Google's own stated Smart Bidding data guidance, as quoted by Vallaeys/Walker (2025): 15 conversions in 30 days.
- Close-variant expansion of exact and phrase match (2019 onward) is why "phrase" in a Hagakure build still matches loosely (Springbok 2022; Geddes 2020 on cross-campaign "most restrictive" matching and duplicate search terms).

## Baseline check
Read `google-ads-account-audit` (Layer 2 structure table: 5–20 keywords per ad group, >25 ad groups flagged, Smart Bidding <30 conv/month flagged) and `google-ads-keyword-cannibalization`. The baseline gives generic structural health ranges and a routing hierarchy. This skill adds: the conversion-volume thresholds as actual study findings (50+ per Optmyzr 14,584-account study; 30 per campaign per Vallaeys/Walker), the explicit rule for when a campaign split is allowed (budget, non-ad-group-level targeting, materially different target), the Hagakure build recipe (one URL per ad group, >1-conversion keyword harvest, DSA backstop with negatives, >3,000 impressions/week landing-page rule), and the Geddes counter-rule that ad-group merging stops at ad/landing-page relevance.

## Judgment calls
- Hagakure originates with Google Japan, so the "method" itself is Google's. The skill therefore treats Hagakure as the *mechanism* and the canon's thresholds and limits as the *decision rules*; the Google contradiction is on broad-match-by-default and on the 15-conversion threshold.
- Lolk is not in the canon. His numbers are cited with that label because they come from his own accounts and were said on a Tier 1 outlet; they do not carry the skill alone.
- Adchieve and Springbok case numbers are reported in the dossier for context but the skill does not rest a rule on them; the rules rest on Optmyzr, Geddes and Bacchini.
- "Mixed keyword harvest" threshold (>1 conversion over 30/60/90 days) comes from the Wenner method description; the skill raises it in line with Lolk's 50–100 click promotion threshold for DSA harvest.

## Canon strength: Strong
Optmyzr study (evidence, Tier 1), Geddes (method + single-account evidence, Tier 1), Vallaeys/Walker (Tier 1 opinion), Bacchini (Tier 2 opinion). Non-canon case studies add directional evidence.

## Invalidated by
Removal of keyword match types or of DSA (AI Max for Search replacing keyword targeting), changes to Smart Bidding's minimum data guidance, or Google making campaign-level data pooling automatic (portfolio by default) — any of these changes the consolidation calculus.
