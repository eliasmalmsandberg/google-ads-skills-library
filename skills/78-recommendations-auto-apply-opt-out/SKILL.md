## What this is
Three things done once and then policed: (1) every auto-apply toggle off, in every account and at manager level; (2) a written list of recommendation types that are dismissed on sight; (3) a daily change-history script that emails when anyone outside the team — including Google's own "Internal Tool" user — changes the account (Rooijmans, 2023–2026). The third part exists because opting out does not stop Google: since June 2024 Google pauses positive Search keywords with zero impressions over 13 months, and low-activity ad groups, "even when you've opted out of all the auto-applies" (Rooijmans, 2024).

## Where it comes from
Rooijmans's audits (80+ accounts) name auto-applied recommendations as the setting that wastes the most money and list the four harmful families (2026); his coaching case gives the numbers for one rep-applied recommendation (2023); his Search Partners case shows auto-apply silently reverting a setting (2023); his change-history script is the monitor (2023, v1.2 2025). Cooney/PPC Hero (2024) documents how the Recommendations UI nudges and which defaults auto-apply re-enables.

## When to use it / when not to
Use when: onboarding any account, inherited or new; after any Google rep call; after adding any new user to the account (another user can switch auto-apply on without your knowledge — Cooney, 2024); when a setting you switched off is back on the next day (Rooijmans, 2023).

Do not use as: a reason to never read the Recommendations tab. Google removed the optimization-score penalty for dismissing, so reading costs nothing (Rooijmans, 2023); some items (disapproved ads, broken URLs, conflicting negatives) are hygiene alerts worth seeing — act on them through your own scripts (skills 73–75), not through the Apply button.

## Inputs
- Admin access to the account and its manager account.
- The list of approved user emails for the change-history script (Rooijmans, 2023).
- A blank Google Sheet and alert email addresses (Rooijmans, 2023).
- Account targets (CPA/ROAS) so budget and bid recommendations can be checked against them before anyone is tempted (Cooney, 2024).

## Method
1. **Opt out, both groups.** Recommendations > Auto-apply: untick every item under both "Maintain your ads" and "Grow your business" [Google docs, unverified grouping]; repeat at manager level, where auto-apply can also be set for sub-accounts [Google docs — unverified in this build]. Path per Cooney (2024): Recommendations > Auto-Apply Recommendations. Record date and who did it.
2. **Standing dismiss list.** Dismiss on sight, every time they reappear: (a) "use broad match" / "add broad match keywords"; (b) bidding-strategy switches, including Target Impression Share with no cap; (c) "add responsive search ads / new creatives" generated by Google; (d) "raise budget" — all four are Rooijmans's harm list (2026). Add (e) "raise bids to first page" — Cooney has never seen Google recommend lowering a bid (2024). The brand-campaign case is the reason for (a)+(b): exact-match manual brand campaign moved to broad + tIS with no cap → brand spend ×3, cost per lead +80%, CRM leads flat (Rooijmans, 2023).
3. **Budget and bid recommendations: arithmetic before action.** Compute the implied CPA of the recommended budget from current CPA; Cooney's example: campaign at $27.07 CPA against a $25 target told to triple budget, implied CPA above $50 (Cooney, 2024). If implied CPA exceeds target, dismiss.
4. **Re-check the defaults auto-apply reverts.** After opting out, verify on every campaign: Search Partners and Display Network off on Search; location "presence" not "presence or interest"; PMax final URL expansion and text-asset generation reviewed; account-level automated assets reviewed; optimized targeting off on Display ad groups unless chosen; new campaigns' default broad match switched off (Cooney, 2024). Search Partners is the one documented to flip back on by itself (Rooijmans, 2023).
5. **Install the change-history alert.** Rooijmans's script queries `change_event` for YESTERDAY, keeps rows whose user email is not in IGNORE_USERS, writes them to the sheet (9,999-row cap) and emails the count (Rooijmans, 2023). Add "Bulk Actions" to IGNORE_USERS only if you run Automated Rules you trust; do not add Google's internal/system users — those are the ones you want to see (Rooijmans, 2023–2024). Schedule daily.
6. **Triage alerts.** Any change by an unlisted user or by Google's system: compare to strategy; revert if it harms (Rooijmans reverted an auto-paused keyword he needed for a promotion, 2024). Any "applied recommendation" entry from a team member goes into the onboarding note as training.
7. **Rep calls.** Treat a rep's suggestion as a recommendation: nothing applied on the call; anything worth testing goes into an experiment (Rooijmans: "Always Be Testing", 2023).
8. **Quarterly**: re-open the auto-apply page and the manager-level page; Google adds new recommendation types and new users may have toggled them (Cooney, 2024).

## What Google will tell you instead, and why we don't
Google frames auto-apply as "AI-powered efficiency" and reps routinely ask for it to be enabled (Rooijmans, 2026; Cooney, 2024). The Partner badge used to require a 70% optimization score, which only recommendations could raise (Rooijmans, 2023). We opt out of all of it: the recommendations push spend up, not results — budget recommendations only ever raise budgets and bid recommendations only ever raise bids (Cooney, 2024) — and the one documented rep-applied change cost a client 80% on cost per lead for zero extra leads (Rooijmans, 2023). We also do not trust the opt-out itself, because Google's low-activity bulk changes ignore it (Rooijmans, 2024); the change-history script is the actual control.

## Failure modes
- **Agencies holding a Partner badge**: the 70% optimization score condition still has to be met across the manager (Rooijmans, 2023); meet it by dismissing (no penalty) rather than applying.
- **Accounts with heavy Automated Rules**: the "Bulk Actions" user floods the alert; whitelist it only after reviewing each rule (Rooijmans, 2023).
- **Accounts with 10k+ changes a day** (large feeds, API tools): the script's 9,999-row cap truncates; run it per campaign label or use Rooijmans's advanced version (Rooijmans, 2023).
- **Rep-managed changes that bypass change history**: Rooijmans reports rep changes made through an internal system that do not appear in change history (2024) — the script cannot see those; watch the inbox for Google's change emails.
- **Tiny low-activity accounts**: Google's 13-month zero-impression keyword pausing will hit most of the keyword list; decide in advance whether to re-enable or accept it (Rooijmans, 2024).
- **New users added by the client**: they can re-enable auto-apply; step 8 is the only defence (Cooney, 2024).

## What to measure
- Count of auto-apply toggles on (target zero) at account and manager level, checked quarterly.
- Change-history alerts per week by user class: team / client / Google system / unknown.
- Recommendations applied (from change history) per month — target zero outside experiments.
- Settings reverted by Google per quarter (Search Partners, low-activity pauses).

## Open disagreements in the canon
Rooijmans says ignore recommendations and test your own opportunities (2023, 2026); Cooney says review the tab regularly and evaluate budget and bid items against target CPA (2024). Epiminds follows Cooney on reading (it is free since dismissal no longer costs score) and Rooijmans on applying (never unreviewed, never outside an experiment). No canon source argues for any auto-apply category; the "Maintain your ads" group (e.g., fixing broken URLs, removing conflicting negatives) is handled by our own scripts so that the fix is logged and reversible.
