## Sources

1. **Source**: Frederick Vallaeys, Optmyzr — "Broad Match is winning the budget war. Is it winning on performance too?", https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/google-ads-match-type-performance/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2026-05-11 (updated 2026-06-18) · **Claim**: Across 30,000 accounts (Feb 2026 data; spend trend 2022–2026): exact match spend share fell ~10 points as broad became the largest match type by budget. Non-brand: exact leads on efficiency; broad produces most volume but overspends on non-converting traffic; phrase takes the largest non-brand spend share and out-converts its cost share. E-commerce: exact's ROAS/CPA lead widens; phrase has the highest CVR of any match type; broad ≈ phrase on ROAS. Lead gen: phrase dominates spend and conversion share; broad loses most ground, attributed to Smart Bidding lacking value signals. Brand: exact leads CTR, conversion share and ROAS; phrase CVR 8.22% vs exact 8.18%, phrase ~36% better than broad. Guardrails: exact for high-intent/high-value; phrase as workhorse; broad for discovery only when Smart Bidding has rich signals; maintained negatives; scrutinise non-exact on brand. Broad's growth reflects comfort with automation, not performance. · **Evidence**: 30k-account study; most CPA/ROAS values only in chart images (not extractable); bid strategy segmentation not reported.
2. **Source**: Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "5 steps you should take before using broad match keywords", https://adalysis.com/blog/6-steps-you-should-take-before-using-broad-match-keywords/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2024-04-30 · **Claim**: Broad uses recent searches, landing page content and other keywords in the ad group — so a shared services page or mixed ad group poisons matching (plumber example). Check impression share first: high IS → expand; low IS → fix worst keywords or convert broad to exact/phrase. Never use broad without conversion tracking or on manual bids; use tCPA/tROAS rather than Max Conversions/Max Value because max strategies spend the whole budget for few extra conversions at larger budgets. Review search terms and run n-grams. Exceptions: tiny/niche markets with no volume; max strategies when data is too thin for targets; don't change what works. Across thousands of accounts CTR and CVR rank exact > phrase > broad excluding brand. Google pushes broad "regardless of circumstances"; Google reps' advice to consolidate ad groups into few RSAs with big keyword lists is a main cause of broad match problems. · **Evidence**: client observations; no study tables.
3. **Source**: Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "How using broad match can lower your CTR as your Quality Score increases", https://adalysis.com/blog/how-using-broad-match-can-lower-your-ctr-as-your-quality-score-increases/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2019-04-23 · **Claim**: With bigger budget and higher QS, a broad keyword matches a wider, looser query set, CTR falls, top/abs-top IS falls and bottom IS rises; when QS drops the set contracts and CTR recovers. Keep broad campaigns separate from campaigns used for IS trend reading. · **Evidence**: illustrative; no numbers.
4. **Source**: Frederick Vallaeys, Optmyzr — "When to stop using Smart Bidding", https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/when-to-stop-using-smart-bidding/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2026-05-28 · **Claim**: tCPA/tROAS need ~50 conversions per 30 days to stabilise; below that Smart Bidding is unreliable; Google's learning requirements have not fallen; Smart Bidding cannot see LTV/margin/deal size. · **Evidence**: internal research across 14,000+ accounts (method not described).
5. **Source**: Nils Rooijmans — "Google Ads Script: Negative Keyword Suggestions", https://nilsrooijmans.com/google-ads-script-negative-keyword-suggestions/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2024-10-21 · **Claim**: Weekly automated negative candidates (clicks > 60, cost > 5, conv < 0.5, 90 days, excluding already-negated); broad match is "pushed aggressively", matching is "far from perfect" and changes often. · **Evidence**: script.

7. **Source**: Navah Hopkins, SEJ — "Match-type changes & campaign structure choices", https://www.searchenginejournal.com/match-type-changes-campaign-structure-choices/352648/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2020-03-10 · **Claim**: Broad to gather data only when the keyword is long-tail (5+ words), alone in its ad group, with all other keywords added as exact and phrase negatives; broad on a brand term is "a recipe for disaster"; single words on anything but exact are wasteful. · **Evidence**: reasoning.
8. **Source**: Navah Hopkins, SEJ — "Why have my Google Ads not got any impressions?", https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ask-a-ppc-why-have-my-google-ads-not-got-any-impressions/515178/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2024-06-13 · **Claim**: Use "limited broad match with many negatives" and careful conversion action selection because broad match factors in conversions; pair discovery with bid caps. · **Evidence**: opinion.

## Dissenting view
McNair/PPC Mastery (2026) runs broad + AI Max + Smart Bidding + negatives as the default bottom-of-funnel setup; Vallaeys (2026) and Geddes (2024) say exact still wins on efficiency and broad is for discovery under conditions. The disagreement is about what "default" means: PPC Mastery treats negatives and Smart Bidding as sufficient guardrails for broad from day one; Optmyzr/Adalysis require evidence of signal quality (conversion value, ≥50 conv/30d) before broad gets budget.

## What the platform currently does [Google docs]
- [Google docs] Broad match is the default match type; may use recent searches, landing page content and other keywords in the ad group; "should be used with Smart Bidding"; broader types include everything narrower types catch (answer/7478529, fetched 2026-10-13). Phrase/BMM change rolled out Feb–Jul 2021 (Geddes 2021). AI Max is grouped with phrase/broad in keyword prioritisation; DSA campaigns auto-upgrade to AI Max in February 2027 (DSA help page answer/2471185, fetched 2026-10-13).
- [Google docs] Keyword prioritisation: identical exact keyword beats broad/phrase/AI Max/PMax; then relevance; then Ad Rank (answer/7478529).

## Judgment calls
- Google's own broad-match uplift studies could not be fetched; the skill states Google's position from the help page and practitioner descriptions of it.
- Optmyzr's chart values were not extractable; the skill uses only the findings stated in text.
- The 50 conversions/30 days figure is from Vallaeys' Smart Bidding piece (2026) and is applied here as the entry gate for broad, since both Vallaeys (2026) and Geddes (2024) condition broad on Smart Bidding having enough signal.
- Position taken: Epiminds follows Optmyzr/Adalysis (gated rollout) over PPC Mastery (broad by default), because the only cross-account evidence in the canon favours exact/phrase on efficiency.

- Review fix (2026-10-13): the 6-week read/rollback window is an Epiminds operating cadence (≈ one Smart Bidding learning period plus a full cycle of the weekly Rooijmans negative script); the 'rising query count with flat conversions' signal is an Epiminds diagnostic rule, labelled as such.
