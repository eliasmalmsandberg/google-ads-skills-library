## Dissenting view
Geddes (2018) concedes some accounts perform as well or better on close variants, and Hopkins (2019) argues looser matching can bring quality intent. The disagreement is not whether leakage exists but whether it is net negative by default; the canon answer is "measure per keyword, decide per keyword", with the burden of proof on the variant.

## What the platform currently does [Google docs]
- [Google docs] Close variants are on for all match types and cannot be opted out. For exact match they include misspellings, singular/plural, stemming, abbreviations, accents, reordered words with the same meaning, added/removed function words, implied words, synonyms/paraphrases and "same search intent" (support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9342105, fetched 2026-10-13; no dates on page). Practitioner sources date the exact-match "same meaning" expansion to Sept 2018 (Geddes 2018; Rooijmans 2018), and the phrase/BMM change to Feb–Jul 2021 (Geddes 2021).
- [Google docs] Search terms report labels "Exact match (close variant)" and "Phrase match (close variant)"; a broad keyword can still produce an "exact match" search term; omits low-activity terms (answer/2472708, fetched 2026-10-13).
- [Google docs] Keyword prioritisation: an exact keyword identical to the query beats phrase/broad/PMax; then relevance "as judged by AI"; then Ad Rank (answer/7478529, fetched 2026-10-13). Geddes (2019, 2021) reports this does not hold reliably in practice.
- [Google docs] Negatives do not match close variants; synonyms and singular/plural must be added manually; misspellings and casing are now handled automatically (answer/2453972, fetched 2026-10-13). The misspelling line post-dates the practitioner sources, which assume misspellings are not covered.

## Baseline check
Read `google-ads-keywords` (one line: "close variants can still match unintended queries — check search terms") and `google-ads-keyword-cannibalization` (match-type overlap). This skill adds the per-keyword split of true-exact vs close-variant performance, the 10% CPA tolerance (Geddes) and 40% share/gap flags (Rooijmans), the duplicate-search-term check, the intent-word examples with measured CVR drops, and the ordered fix list (negatives → phrase → restructure → add the variant as a keyword).

## Judgment calls
- Two numeric flags exist: Geddes' "within 10% CPA" tolerance (2018) and Rooijmans' 40% share/40% gap highlight (2018, maintained 2025). Epiminds uses both: 40% as the screen (which keywords to look at), 10% as the tolerance (when to act), with a minimum-spend floor so tiny keywords don't trigger work (Geddes 2018: weigh against share of spend).
- Google's newer "misspellings handled automatically" for negatives is recorded but not relied on; the audit still checks misspelled variants explicitly.

## Canon strength: Strong
Three Geddes posts with account-level data on close-variant CVR/CPA, Rooijmans' published script with thresholds, Hopkins on structure implications.

## Invalidated by
Google removing the "(close variant)" label from the search terms report; a change to keyword prioritisation that makes identical exact keywords reliably win; negatives starting to match close variants/synonyms; further search-term visibility cuts hiding most variant terms.
