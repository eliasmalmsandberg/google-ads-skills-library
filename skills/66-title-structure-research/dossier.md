## Dissenting view
Vlietstra (ZATO, 2026) raises that AI Max for Shopping's text customisation rewrites titles per query, which could make feed-title structure matter less in Standard Shopping; Williams (2025–2026) still treats the feed title as the primary lever. The disagreement is about whether Google's dynamic titles override the merchant's title often enough to change where effort goes — Google's own spec says it makes no change when the merchant title already best fits the query [Google docs], which favours Williams.

## What the platform currently does [Google docs]
- `title` 1–150 characters; over-limit titles are truncated with a warning. Users usually see only the first ~70 characters or fewer, depending on screen, so key details go first. [Google docs, undated]
- Google's examples: apparel = product name, size, colour ("Sleeveless Polka Dot Skater Dress, Small, Black"); electronics = brand, model, storage, colour, lock status; bundles list all components; subscriptions include "subscription" + duration. [Google docs]
- Prohibited: promotional text (price, sale, shipping, dates, company name), ALL CAPS, symbols, HTML, gimmick foreign words, titles not matching the landing page. [Google docs]
- "Automatic title improvements" / dynamic titles: Google may promote attributes from the landing page, images and other signals into the ad title; no change when the merchant title already best fits the query; opt-out via support, 3–5 business days. AI-generated titles must go in `structured_title` with `digital_source_type = trained_algorithmic_media`; if both exist Google uses `title`. [Google docs]
- AI Max for Shopping (announced 2026-04-30) adds query-matched text customisation in Standard Shopping (Vlietstra/ZATO, 2026, reporting the announcement).

## Judgment calls
- The row's origin is "Industry studies". On inspection the "studies" with big numbers are tool-vendor content without methodology; I excluded them and say so in the skill. The honest finding is that published evidence on title structure is sparse: one 2016 controlled test and the practitioners' method.
- Added CommerceHub/Harlow as Tier 2 for this topic only.
- Treated the Rhodes nGram tab as the research method (what to test), not as findings.

## Canon strength: Adequate
Two Tier 1 practitioners (Williams, Rhodes) with method; one added Tier 2 with a controlled result; no cross-account study that passes the inclusion rule.

## Invalidated by
Google changing the displayed-title cut-off or `title` limit, AI Max / dynamic titles overriding merchant titles by default, or Merchant Center removing attribute rules / supplemental sources (the test mechanism).
