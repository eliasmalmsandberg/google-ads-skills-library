# Dossier — 68 Shopping query sculpting via priority + negatives

## Sources

1. **Source**: Kirk Williams, ZATO, "How to Set Up Google Shopping Query Sculpting in 2026" (republished from his 2015-09-03 Search Engine Land walkthrough; credits Martin Roettgerding's Dec 2014 method), https://zatomarketing.com/blog/google-shopping-query-sculpting · **Tier**: 1 · **Date**: 2021-08-31, updated 2026-04-21; core 2015 · **Claim**: Same products in three campaigns split by query intent, routed by priority + negatives: Non-brand (High priority, low bids e.g. $0.50, negatives = all brand and SKU terms), Brand (Medium, higher bids e.g. $2.00, negatives = SKU/product-name terms), SKU (Low, high bids e.g. $5 up to $10–15, minimal negatives). One shared budget across all three so a higher-priority campaign never drops out of the auction and blocks the lower ones. Negative propagation rule: any negative added to a higher-funnel campaign must also be added to every lower campaign, or the query is filtered down and you pay more for it. Setup: use the SQR to compare "contains brand" vs "does not contain" (example 100 conv @ $40 vs 450 @ $50) and convert the stronger existing campaign into NB to keep history; copy to make BR and SKU, deleting the copied negatives correctly ("will end up destroying your entire strategy" if not); segment ad groups finely with Item ID exclusions and uncheck "all products". 2026 notes: the strategy "does not work with PMax" but "kind of works" since brand can be excluded from PMax and sculpted into Standard Shopping; PMax CPCs typically lower; each tier gets its own ROAS target (NB: manual or tROAS; BR: manual to own brand). Results: traffic and cost rose; CVR +21% and +168% in two manufacturer examples; one decline blamed on a month-long stock-out; AOV sometimes fell while revenue rose. Failure modes: negligible brand traffic (use a conversion-based variant: negate converting queries out of NB into Medium), copy errors, incomplete negatives, slow data, over-segmentation, absurd matches (an exact "m" query). · **Evidence or opinion**: Multi-client anecdotes with two CVR figures; no sample sizes.

2. **Source**: Nils Rooijmans, "[AMA] Google does not respect campaign priorities in my Standard Shopping Campaigns. How come?", https://nilsrooijmans.com/daily/ama-google-does-not-respect-campaign-priorities-in-my-standard-shopping-campaigns-how-come · **Tier**: 1 · **Date**: 2026-03-04 (mod. 2026-06-13) · **Claim**: Priorities are not broken; in his audits "Gatekeeper" setups fail for four reasons: (1) targeting mismatches — device, location, schedule, audience, demographics, language, network, IP exclusions or country-of-sale differ between tiers, so the higher tier can't match and the query drops; (2) inconsistent negatives across tiers; (3) incomplete product coverage — tiers must be eligible for the same product set; (4) budget constraints — a limited or exhausted higher-priority budget lets queries bypass it. · **Evidence or opinion**: Audit experience; no counts.

## Dissenting view
Williams (2026, AI Max account theory) now argues against the default of excluding brand from PMax and sculpting it into Standard Shopping, because ZATO's tests found PMax efficient on brand; his own 2026-updated sculpting guide still recommends the exclusion route. Williams (2026, feed-first) also says a simpler campaign on a strong feed can beat a complex one. The disagreement is about whether the three-tier structure still earns its complexity once PMax owns brand/remarketing and the feed is clean — i.e. when the method's remaining job is non-brand and SKU separation only.

## What the platform currently does [Google docs]
- PMax: Search campaigns with exact-match keywords take priority over PMax for the same query; search themes rank with phrase/broad; a budget-limited Search campaign can let PMax serve on its exact terms. Campaign-level brand exclusions and account- or campaign-level negative keywords are available in PMax; search themes respect both. [Google docs, undated]
- Shopping priority levels High/Medium/Low exist; practitioners report higher priority serves first for matching products regardless of bid (Vallaeys, 2015; Williams, 2015/2026). Google's priority help page could not be fetched for this dossier.
- AI Max for Shopping (announced 2026-04-30) is available in Standard Shopping and adds text customisation, format selection and Final URL expansion (Vlietstra/ZATO, 2026).
- Custom labels take 24–48 hours to reach Google Ads [Google docs, Google Ads Help].

## Baseline check
Read `google-ads-audit-ecommerce` (Layer 3: "High priority: brand + exact product queries… Low priority: generic broad queries; add negatives at each tier to route queries downward"). The baseline has the tiers *inverted* relative to the practitioner method (generic goes High/low-bid, SKU goes Low/high-bid) and has no shared-budget rule, no negative-propagation rule, no targeting-parity rule, no setup sequence and no failure list. This skill supplies the correct direction and the four failure causes Rooijmans finds in audits.

## Judgment calls
- Roettgerding's original (ppc-epiphany.com) could not be fetched; credited via Williams.
- Google's priority help page could not be fetched; the "serves first regardless of bid" mechanic is attributed to Vallaeys/Williams rather than labelled [Google docs].
- Williams' internal inconsistency on BR/SKU negatives (tier table vs step-by-step) is resolved in the skill by the stricter reading: BR negates SKU and product-name terms; SKU negates only proven junk.
- The row says "Established" maturity and "As needed": the skill requires existing SQR history (the setup uses it) and treats the build as one-off with ongoing negative maintenance.

## Canon strength: Strong
Three Tier 1 practitioners (Williams across five pieces 2015–2026, Rooijmans 2026, Vallaeys 2015) with published method, two quantified single-account results and audit-derived failure causes; no cross-account study.

## Invalidated by
Google removing campaign priority from Standard Shopping, forcing PMax precedence over Standard Shopping for the same products without brand exclusions, removing shared budgets, or AI Max for Shopping replacing Standard Shopping's keyword-free matching with its own query rules.
