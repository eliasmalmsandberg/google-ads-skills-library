## Sources

1. **Source**: Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "N-gram analysis: The secret to scalable search term management in Google Ads", https://adalysis.com/blog/n-gram-analysis-the-secret-to-scalable-search-term-management-in-google-ads/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2025-04-16 · **Claim**: Build 1-, 2- and 3-word n-grams with per-n-gram word count and instance count columns; ~1M search terms collapse to 30–50k n-grams. Negative candidates = clicks > 150 and conversions = 0, or conversions = 0 sorted by cost. High-CPA review = conversions > 1, clicks > 150, cost > account-scaled floor, sort by CPA/ROAS. Fractional conversions (DDA) distort n-gram CPA (example: $17,377 CPA on $173 spend, 0.01 conv). Do not negate a theme you could serve — build an ad group instead; negate temporarily for products you don't sell yet. · **Evidence**: account examples ("and leisure"/"lawn and leisure": 265 search terms, 99 clicks, >$1,000, 0 conv; "today" n-gram 1 conv/yr; "wine" demand case). Vendor content, but method and data shown.
2. **Source**: Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "Using search term data to inform negative keyword or new product decisions", https://adalysis.com/blog/using-search-query-data-inform-negative-keyword-new-product-decisions/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2017-10-10 · **Claim**: Filter n-grams to < 2 conversions, sort by cost desc; prefer one root negative over many term-level negatives; check whether a losing n-gram is unmet demand before negating. · **Evidence**: "napkins" in 1,804 queries at 4% CVR; "wine" in 99 queries, 1 conv at very high CPA, no single term > 60 clicks — invisible at term level, obvious at n-gram level.
3. **Source**: Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "Analyze your query data in a brand new way: n-grams", https://adalysis.com/blog/analyze-your-query-data-in-a-brand-new-way-n-grams/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2016-06-15 · **Claim**: Aggregating 1/2/3-grams exposes patterns invisible per term; "in [city]" variants at 10 clicks/0 conv each aggregate to 60 clicks/0 conv; run weekly because of compute cost. · **Evidence**: worked example table.
4. **Source**: Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "Working with Google's broad match modifier / phrase match changes", https://adalysis.com/blog/working-with-googles-broad-match-modifier-phrase-match-changes/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2021-02-05 · **Claim**: Use n-grams to find intent words that move CPA; either negate them or add them as separate keywords at lower bids. · **Evidence**: account at ~$450 CPA: "how" queries > 2× that, "quote" < $300, cost-question queries > $1,000.
5. **Source**: Nils Rooijmans — "Updated Google Ads script: Brainlabs' Search Query Mining for n-gram analysis", https://nilsrooijmans.com/updated-google-ads-script-brainlabs-search-query-mining-for-n-gram-analysis/ · **Tier** 1 (Rooijmans; script originally Brainlabs, Tier 1) · **Date** 2022-08-22, modified 2025-05-27 · **Claim**: Full script published. Defaults: n-gram length 1–2, min impressions 10, checkNegatives=true (drops queries already blocked by ad group, campaign or shared-list negatives), ignores paused campaigns/ad groups, output tabs per n length at account/campaign/ad group level plus a word-count (query length 1…7+) tab; rows sorted cost desc. Good performers → new ad groups; poor performers → phrase-match negative. · **Evidence**: script code; no performance study.
6. **Source**: Nils Rooijmans — "Google Ads Script: Negative Keyword Suggestions", https://nilsrooijmans.com/google-ads-script-negative-keyword-suggestions/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2024-10-21, modified 2025-05-09 · **Claim**: Term-level negative candidates = clicks > 60, cost > 5, conversions < 0.5 over LAST_90_DAYS, excluding terms already covered by any negative level; run weekly (Monday). States Google's matching is "far from perfect" and broad match is pushed aggressively. · **Evidence**: script; opinion on Google.
7. **Source**: Geetanjali Tyagi, Optmyzr Help — "Search Term N-Grams user guide", https://help.optmyzr.com/en/articles/3075385-search-term-n-grams-user-guide · **Tier** 1 outlet (Optmyzr), tool documentation · **Date** 2026-07-15 · **Claim**: N-grams up to 6 words; filter "queries without conversions"; click-through to underlying queries before negating; AI suggestions must be reviewed. 8. **Source**: Navah Hopkins, Search Engine Journal — "Should I use broad or phrase match negative?", https://www.searchenginejournal.com/broad-or-phrase-match-negative/453329/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2022-06-15 · **Claim**: Single-word negatives are identical on broad and phrase in Google; default to phrase (imports to Microsoft); use 2–3-word negatives when a single word would block relevant traffic (example "support" vs "support number"). · **Evidence**: opinion with examples.
9. **Source**: Vimal Bharadwaj, Optmyzr — "Protect Smart Bidding from spam", https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/protect-smart-bidding-from-spam/ · **Tier** 1 outlet · **Date** 2026-07-21 · **Claim**: Negating a real high-intent term can do more damage than the spam it blocks; a negative does not un-count conversions Smart Bidding already learned from. · **Evidence**: anecdote; vendor content.

## Dissenting view
Threshold disagreement: Rooijmans' term-level script flags at > 60 clicks and < 0.5 conversions (2024); Geddes' n-gram rule flags at > 150 clicks and 0 conversions (2025). The disagreement is about unit of analysis (term vs n-gram) and about how much data is "enough" before blocking. On the action itself, Geddes (2025) dissents from reflexive negation: a losing n-gram may be unmet demand or a servable theme and should become an ad group or a temporary negative, not a permanent block; Optmyzr (2026) adds that a wrong negative costs more than the waste it stops.

## What the platform currently does [Google docs]
- [Google docs] Search terms report omits terms "that don't have enough query activity" for privacy; low-volume queries are folded into insight themes or "other queries" (support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472708, fetched 2026-10-13; no change dates on page). Practitioner memory places the big visibility cut in September 2020 — not verifiable from the page.
- [Google docs] Negative keywords: broad = all words any order, phrase = words in order, exact = whole query; "won't match to close variants or other expansions" — synonyms and singular/plural must be added manually; casing and misspellings "handled automatically" (answer/2453972, fetched 2026-10-13). The misspelling line is newer than the practitioner sources above, which say negatives do not cover misspellings (Geddes 2017; Hopkins 2020).
- [Google docs] PMax search terms: campaign-level negatives via Keywords > Negative keywords; account-level PMax negative list up to 1,000 keywords; asset groups take no negatives (per Hopkins/Optmyzr 2026-09-25 summarising Google docs).

## Baseline check
Read `google-ads-search-term-mining` and `google-ads-negative-keywords`. Baseline covers term-level sorting by cost, generic thresholds ("$50 with 0 conversions"), and a one-line mention of "pattern clustering" by modifier. This skill adds the n-gram unit of analysis with published thresholds (Geddes 150 clicks; Rooijmans 60 clicks), the instance-count and word-count columns, the fractional-conversion trap, the "build an ad group, don't negate" rule, the published script defaults, and the root-negative principle.

## Judgment calls
- Epiminds adopts Geddes' n-gram thresholds for blocking (clicks > 150, conv = 0) and Rooijmans' lower thresholds (clicks > 60) only as a review queue, not an auto-block. Reason: a root negative blocks many queries at once, so the cost of a wrong n-gram negative is higher than a wrong term negative.
- Optmyzr help centre is tool documentation; cited only for scope (n up to 6) and the "review before negating" rule, not for thresholds.
- Brainlabs original post unreachable; attributed as "Brainlabs script via Rooijmans".

- Review fix (2026-10-13): the ~1,000 search terms/month floor is an Epiminds operating threshold (derived from Geddes' 150-click block rule), and the long-cycle lead-gen conversion-lag caution is Epiminds practice; both now labelled as unsourced in SKILL.md.

## Canon strength: Strong
Four Geddes posts with account data, two Rooijmans scripts with defaults, Hopkins on negative match type, Optmyzr on the cost of wrong negatives.

## Invalidated by
