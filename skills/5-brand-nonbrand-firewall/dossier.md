# Dossier — 5 Brand/non-brand firewalling (negative mesh)

## Sources

1. **Source**: Nils Rooijmans, nilsrooijmans.com daily, "[AMA] Do you use brand inclusions/exclusions in PMax campaigns?" — https://nilsrooijmans.com/daily/ama-do-you-use-brand-inclusions-exclusions-in-pmax-campaigns · **Tier**: 1 · **Date**: 2025-12-03 (updated 2025-12-19) · **Claim**: Uses PMax brand exclusions only where Google recognises the brand, and *always* adds a negative keyword list with the brand name, every spelling variant and typo, because PMax search terms still show typo variants after a brand exclusion; competitor brands are excluded with negatives too because Google often does not recognise competitors as brands; brand inclusions/exclusions are not supported in Standard Shopping (the option appearing there was a Google bug, per a retracted article). · **Evidence or opinion**: Practitioner method from his own accounts; no performance numbers.

2. **Source**: Nils Rooijmans, "[AMA] How do you run BRAND campaigns in Shopping?" — https://nilsrooijmans.com/daily/ama-how-do-you-run-brand-campaigns-in-shopping · **Tier**: 1 · **Date**: 2025-11-19 (updated 2026-01-26) · **Claim**: Brand Shopping campaign at *low* priority with high manual CPC (or a strategy allowing high CPCs); brand terms in a negative list attached to all non-brand Shopping campaigns (high/medium priority); every product in the brand campaign must also exist in the non-brand campaigns; the non-brand campaigns must never run out of budget before the brand campaign, otherwise non-brand queries fall through to the brand campaign; keep schedule/audience/device/location identical across them. Explicitly rejects the inverse (brand campaign broad, script negatives out non-brand) as losing money and hitting negative-list limits. · **Evidence or opinion**: Method; opinion from accounts.

3. **Source**: Nils Rooijmans, "Brand restrictions for Search campaigns" — https://nilsrooijmans.com/daily/brand-restrictions-for-search-campaigns · **Tier**: 1 · **Date**: 2023-12-18 · **Claim**: Brand restrictions (now brand inclusions) require the broad match campaign setting; he has not tested them and prefers a script that retroactively adds non-brand search terms from the brand campaign as negatives. · **Evidence or opinion**: Opinion/method.

4. **Source**: Nils Rooijmans, "Google Ads Script Review – PMax Brand Traffic Analyzer" — https://nilsrooijmans.com/google-ads-script-review-pmax-brand-traffic-analyzer/ · **Tier**: 1 (review of a smec script) · **Date**: 2024-06-03 · **Claim**: PMax goes after warm bottom-funnel traffic and takes credit for it, so branded conversions can mask wasteful non-brand spend; chart brand vs non-brand share of PMax conversions from the search-term insights; if brand dominates, exclude brand and close variants via a shared negative list, ideally auto-populated by script. · **Evidence or opinion**: Method; no measured results.

5. **Source**: Manuel Baudisch, smec (Smarter Ecommerce), "Is PMax eating your brand search?" — https://smarter-ecommerce.com/blog/en/google-ads/pmax-brand-script/ · **Tier**: Not canon (vendor script post) — technical caveats only · **Date**: 2024-09-12 · **Claim**: Many PMax search terms come back "uncategorized" (blank) from the API; author estimates >90% of those are non-brand but cannot guarantee it; brand matching is CONTAINS, not exact; script does not run at MCC level. · **Evidence or opinion**: Technical description.

6. **Source**: Fred Vallaeys with Shawn Walker, Optmyzr PPC Town Hall — https://www.optmyzr.com/ppctownhall/practical-advice-on-how-to-actually-make-smart-bidding-work-for-you · **Tier**: 1 · **Date**: 2025-01-29 · **Claim**: The one campaign split that always qualifies is brand vs non-brand, because they perform differently enough to need different targets; otherwise split themes into ad groups, not campaigns. · **Evidence or opinion**: Opinion.

7. **Source**: Brad Geddes (Adalysis), "Google Ads Account Organization" workshop slides — https://presentations.boussiasevents.gr/files/_boussias_conferences_content/presentations/google_marketing_conference/2020/day2/workshop_brad_geddes_googleMKT_20.pdf · **Tier**: 1 · **Date**: 2020 · **Claim**: Brand and exact campaigns can take maximum budget; across campaigns Google tries to serve the "most restrictive" match but rotates among matches over time, which is why duplicate search terms appear across campaigns — the firewall has to be negatives, not reliance on Google's preference; bid brand on target impression share. · **Evidence or opinion**: Method, with cross-campaign matching behaviour from observation.

8. **Source**: Maggie Humphrey (Cypress North), Search Engine Land, "Separating brand and non-brand campaigns…" — https://searchengineland.com/separating-brand-non-brand-campaigns-roas-484080 · **Tier**: Not canon (agency) — evidence noted · **Date**: 2026-07-31 · **Claim**: Blended automation favours brand because it is cheap and converts, creating a feedback loop; she split brand out, cut brand to a small share of spend, rebuilt non-brand as Standard Shopping, and used non-brand Search keywords as exact negatives in the brand Shopping campaign plus an aggressive tROAS and sometimes a portfolio max-CPC cap. Result (one client, YoY): Google PPC revenue −25% (~−$2.3M), organic +99%, combined +15%, new customers +20%. · **Evidence or opinion**: Single-account YoY, no control.

9. **Source**: JumpFly blog, "Target ROAS for branded campaigns: don't do it" — https://www.jumpfly.com/blog/target-roas-for-branded-campaigns-dont-do-it/ · **Tier**: Not canon (agency) — context · **Date**: undated · **Claim**: Brand traffic is uniformly high-value so tROAS has nothing to learn from; lowering a brand tROAS raised CPC with no gain in impressions or conversions; run brand on eCPC/impression-share. · **Evidence or opinion**: Agency anecdote.

10. **Source**: Search Engine Land (contributed, unbylined), "Google Ads brand settings: inclusion & exclusion" — https://searchengineland.com/google-ads-brand-settings-452578 · **Tier**: Reference only (describes Google feature) · **Date**: undated (feature era 2023–24) · **Claim**: Brand inclusion applies only to Search campaigns with broad match; brand exclusion applies to Search and PMax (Shopping+Search inventory or Search only); exclusions are not perfect and negatives are still needed; PMax allows only up to 100 negative keywords per campaign at the time of writing. · **Evidence or opinion**: Feature description.

## Dissenting view
Google's own direction (brand inclusions/exclusions, and the "Smart Bidding handles the mix" position) is the dissent: it says the brand list is the firewall. Nils (2025) and the SEL feature write-up both show the exclusion leaks typos and unrecognised competitor brands, so negatives remain the primary wall. A second disagreement is the direction of the Shopping firewall: Nils puts brand at *low* priority and routes with negatives on non-brand; Humphrey puts non-brand keywords as negatives *in the brand* campaign. The disagreement is about which side carries the negative list — Nils's reason (negative-list limits and broad matching) is the stronger one.

## What the platform currently does [Google docs]
- Brand restrictions for broad-match Search campaigns launched 2023 and were renamed brand inclusions; brand exclusions are available for Search and PMax (Shopping + Search inventory, or Search only) (SEL brand-settings write-up; Nils 2023). [Google docs, via SEL]
- Brand inclusion requires the campaign's broad match setting (Nils, 2023). [Google docs]
- PMax campaign-level negative keywords were limited to 100 per campaign in the period covered by the SEL write-up; Nils (2025) reports brand lists are not supported in Standard Shopping. [Google docs]
- Cross-campaign serving: Google prefers the "most restrictive" matching keyword across campaigns but rotates over time (Geddes, 2020, observation — not a Google doc).
- PMax search-term insights are available but many terms return uncategorised (smec, 2024).

## Baseline check
Read `google-ads-negative-keywords` (Source 2: brand variations as negatives in non-brand; brand campaign gets generic negatives) and `google-ads-keyword-cannibalization` (Type 4 brand leakage; routing hierarchy). The baseline states the principle. This skill adds: the full mesh across Search, Shopping and PMax; the typo/variant rule that makes brand-list exclusions insufficient (Nils); the Shopping direction (brand low priority, negatives on non-brand, budget-never-exhausts rule); the PMax brand-share diagnostic and its API caveats; the bid-strategy position for brand (impression share, not tROAS); and the measurement framing that brand leakage inflates blended ROAS.

## Judgment calls
- Only one Tier 1 practitioner (Nils) publishes an explicit mesh method; Vallaeys/Walker and Geddes supply the split rationale and cross-campaign behaviour. Canon strength is therefore Adequate, not Strong.
- Humphrey's case is non-canon and used only as evidence that blended automation leans brand; her negative direction is noted as the alternative and rejected.
- "Negative mesh" in the row name is read as the full set of cross-campaign negatives/exclusions across Search, Shopping and PMax, not only Search.
- Web search budget was exhausted mid-research; Optmyzr/Adalysis index pages were fetched but showed no brand-specific posts in their current listings.

- Post-review: the leakage-review cadence (weekly until clean, then monthly), the zero-leak target, the generic-word-brand rule (exact negative on the full phrase) and the reseller rule (manufacturer brands stay off List A) are Epiminds derived rules with no canon source, labelled as such in the skill.

## Canon strength: Adequate
Three Tier 1 sources (Nils ×4 posts, Vallaeys/Walker, Geddes) with method; evidence is single-account and anecdotal.

## Invalidated by
Google making brand exclusions catch typos/variants reliably and extending brand lists to Standard Shopping; removal of campaign-level negatives in PMax; AI Max replacing keyword-based Search campaigns so negatives no longer gate serving.
