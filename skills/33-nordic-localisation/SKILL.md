STATUS: THIN CANON — needs practitioner input

## What the dossier found
No Tier 1 or Tier 2 canon member publishes a method or evidence on Nordic-language ad copy or on localisation versus translation. The only adjacent evidence is English-language and not language-segmented: Optmyzr's 2026 ~20,000-account study (headlines under 20 characters had CPA $9.35 vs $18.27; sentence-case headlines outperformed title case, with the author warning the matched sample was 31 accounts and telling readers to test locally) and Hopkins' 2024 1M-ad study (sentence case beat title case for RSAs). Title case is an English convention, so the case finding may not even be definable in Swedish, Danish, Norwegian or Finnish copy. The platform facts that do apply: the 30/90/15 limits are per-language with no double-width allowance for Nordic letters (Google Ads Help), a keyword over 30 characters should go in a description (relevant to long compound nouns), and `{LOCATION}` and `{GLOBAL_COUNTDOWN}` exist for per-market insertion (Google Ads API docs; see skill 29). Brad Geddes' 2026 label-based cross-ad-group test method is the right instrument for comparing a localised set with a translated set, but no one in the canon has published such a test.

## What is missing
- Any published comparison of translated vs. locally written copy in a Nordic market, with numbers.
- Per-language headline-length distributions (compound nouns in Swedish and Finnish eat the 30-character limit; does the under-20-character finding hold?).
- Local CTA and trust-signal conventions (formal/informal address, price display with "kr"/"€", VAT inclusion, local review platforms, Swish/MobilePay/Vipps mentions) with evidence rather than convention.
- Whether English-language headlines in Nordic SERPs (common in SaaS and tech) win or lose against local-language lines.
- Policy edge cases: Finnish/Swedish bilingual requirements in Finland; Norwegian Bokmål vs Nynorsk.
