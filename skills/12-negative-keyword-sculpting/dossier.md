## Sources

1. **Source**: Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "Should you remove redundant keywords?", https://adalysis.com/blog/should-you-remove-redundant-keywords/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2024-07-03 · **Claim**: Dismiss Google's "remove redundant keywords" recommendation. Observed serving rules: an identical exact keyword is set aside and usually enters the auction; the same keyword in several campaigns serves from the most restricted campaign (lower budget, fewer devices, smaller geo); the same keyword in several ad groups serves from the highest Ad Rank; no match → Google picks anything "close enough". Keep exact alongside phrase/broad; add brand misspellings as keywords; use negatives to manage overlap, including PMax. · **Evidence**: one search term served by 12 ad groups with CPA $24–$43; brand term 53% CTR, >40% CVR, $0.23 CPA while misspelled brand queries leaked to PMax; Google's "corrects misspellings before matching" did not hold.
2. **Source**: Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "How to steer the machine with negative keywords", https://adalysis.com/blog/how-to-steer-the-machine-with-negative-keywords/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2022-05-17 · **Claim**: Workflow: n-grams first, then duplicate search terms across ad groups, then conflicts. Google does not reliably serve the ad group with the exactly matching keyword for variants (misspellings, plurals, word order). Google's conflict recommendations ignore match types and campaign lists. · **Evidence**: advertiser spending millions/month on exact + tCPA, account CPA ~$300, "how" queries > $1,000 CPA; Smart Bidding "never" adjusted for them; 10,000+ clicks in 90 days as enough data; duplicate ad groups converting 10% vs 1%.
3. **Source**: Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "A negative keyword primer", https://adalysis.com/blog/negative-keyword-primer-ppc/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2017-02-02 · **Claim**: Negatives flow downward; an ad-group negative blocks only in that ad group and the query can show through another ("negative whacking"); campaign negative blocks the campaign; shared list for several campaigns. Match type only affects word order; single-word broad = single-word phrase; negatives don't match singular/plural/misspellings (2017). · **Evidence**: worked plasma-TV examples.
4. **Source**: Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "Find and fix common negative keyword problems", https://adalysis.com/blog/find-fix-common-negative-keyword-problems-ppc-accounts/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2017-02-23 · **Claim**: Inconsistent per-campaign negatives (112/894/396/112 across nine campaigns) → move account-wide negatives to shared lists, separate lists only where campaign types differ (brand vs non-brand); don't repeat the same list in every ad group; "+" copied into negatives is malformed; 10–50 clicks/0 conv is too little data. · **Evidence**: account examples.
5. **Source**: Brad Geddes, Adalysis — "Is Performance Max cannibalizing your Search ads?", https://adalysis.com/blog/is-performance-max-cannibalizing-your-search-ads-a-deep-dive-into-search-term-overlap/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2024-12-12 · **Claim**: Export PMax search terms, add top performers as exact Search keywords; ignore "remove redundant keywords"; Google's docs on Search vs PMax priority are internally inconsistent. · **Evidence**: 3,300+ non-retail PMax campaigns, ~1.2M terms (>10 impressions): 45% of PMax terms also appear as Search terms; where overlap exists PMax has more impressions 61% of the time; Search has higher CTR 65%, higher CVR 84.18%, higher conv value 84.62% of the time.
6. **Source**: Navah Hopkins, SEJ — "How to use negative keywords in paid search campaigns", https://www.searchenginejournal.com/negative-keywords-paid-search-campaigns/365834/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2020-05-12 · **Claim**: Brand and competitor terms as negatives in generic campaigns (brand is cheaper and converts better, distorting generic CPA); for DSA add all actively targeted keywords as negatives — contradicting Google's position that DSA won't compete with keywords; 10,000 negatives/campaign cap; negatives don't account for close variants; prefer phrase for single words (Microsoft import). · **Evidence**: author's DSA observation; no numbers.
7. **Source**: Navah Hopkins, SEJ — "Should I use broad or phrase match negative?", https://www.searchenginejournal.com/broad-or-phrase-match-negative/453329/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2022-06-15 · **Claim**: Single-word negatives keep ad groups from competing ("software" and "platform" each as negative in the other's ad group); because close variants can make two ad groups bid against each other, use single-word negatives across them. · **Evidence**: examples.
8. **Source**: Nils Rooijmans — "How to add negative keywords to your Performance Max campaigns", https://nilsrooijmans.com/how-to-add-negative-keywords-to-your-performance-max-campaigns/ · **Tier** 1 · **Date** 2025-01-03 · **Claim**: Prefer a shared negative list for PMax over account-level negatives (account-level also hits non-PMax campaigns); per-campaign PMax negatives were beta at time of writing. · **Evidence**: method.

## Dissenting view
Google's own recommendation (remove redundant keywords; broader match types "include everything narrower types catch") is the dissent: it says routing by negatives is unnecessary because one broad keyword covers the exact and phrase versions. Geddes (2024) and the PMax overlap study (2024) reject this with data on CPA spread across ad groups and PMax winning impressions while losing CVR. Within the canon, Hopkins (SEJ, 2020) argues against match-type-split campaigns because they multiply negatives — a disagreement about how many campaigns should exist, not about whether routing negatives are needed.

## What the platform currently does [Google docs]
- [Google docs] Keyword prioritisation: identical exact keyword > phrase/broad/AI Max/PMax search theme identical to query > relevance "as judged by AI" > Ad Rank. Keywords may serve from PMax instead of Search when the Search campaign is budget-limited, the keyword is low search volume, creatives/landing pages are disapproved, or targeting isn't met (answer/7478529, fetched 2026-10-13). Near-duplicate broad keywords are treated as duplicates with the higher Ad Rank serving.
- [Google docs] Negatives: broad = all words any order; phrase = in order; exact = whole query; no close variants/expansions; synonyms and plurals manual; misspellings and casing automatic; account-level list applies to Search and Shopping inventory (answer/2453972, fetched 2026-10-13).
- [Google docs] PMax: campaign-level negatives via Keywords > Negative keywords with impact preview; account-level PMax negative list up to 1,000 keywords; asset groups take no negatives; brand exclusions separate from negatives (Hopkins/Optmyzr 2026-09-25 summarising Google docs).

## Judgment calls
- Campaign-level routing (which campaign owns a query) and ad-group-level routing (which ad group serves a term) are treated as one skill because the fix is the same tool at two levels; the hierarchy order is Epiminds' synthesis of Geddes (2024) and Hopkins (2020).
- The "most restricted campaign wins" serving rule is a Geddes observation (2024), not Google-documented; the skill labels it as such.

- Review fix (2026-10-13): the 2–4-week verification window is an Epiminds operating cadence (no canon source); the 10,000 clicks/90 days routing floor is an Epiminds adaptation of Geddes' 2022 'enough for a machine to learn' figure and is labelled as such.

## Canon strength: Strong
Five Geddes posts (two with account data, one with a 3,300-campaign study), two Hopkins posts, one Rooijmans method.

## Invalidated by
Google changing keyword prioritisation so identical keywords always win regardless of campaign restriction; negatives matching close variants; removal of campaign-level PMax negatives; AI Max replacing keyword-level control in Search campaigns (DSA auto-upgrade to AI Max scheduled Feb 2027 per Google docs).
